Buying Your First Condo in Singapore: A Complete Guide

May 2026 · INITIUM Team · 8 min read

Buying a condominium in Singapore is one of the biggest financial decisions most people will make. Whether you are a young professional looking for your first home or a couple planning for the future, the process can feel overwhelming. This guide breaks it down into clear, actionable steps.

1. Check Your Eligibility

Before you start browsing listings, understand what you are eligible to buy. Singapore citizens, Permanent Residents, and foreigners each face different rules when purchasing private property.

  • Singapore Citizens: Can buy any condo with no restrictions. No Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty (ABSD) for the first property.
  • Permanent Residents: Can buy condos but pay 5% ABSD on the first property.
  • Foreigners: Can buy condos but pay 60% ABSD. Not eligible for Executive Condominiums (ECs) until they become PRs.

2. Know Your Numbers

Work out what you can afford before you fall in love with a unit. The Total Debt Servicing Ratio (TDSR) limits your monthly debt repayments to 55% of your gross monthly income. This includes your car loan, student loans, credit cards, and the new mortgage.

A good rule of thumb: your monthly mortgage should not exceed 30% of your monthly income. Factor in these costs:

  • Down payment: Minimum 25% for a first property (5% in cash, the rest from CPF or cash).
  • Buyer's Stamp Duty (BSD): 1% on the first $180,000, 2% on the next $180,000, 3% on the next $640,000, and 4% on the remainder.
  • Legal fees: $2,500–$4,000.
  • Agent commission: 1% for resale (paid by buyer).
  • Renovation: Budget $30,000–$100,000 depending on the condition.

3. Get Your IPA First

An In-Principle Approval (IPA) from a bank tells you exactly how much they will lend you. Without it, you are flying blind. Sellers and agents take you more seriously when you have one. Shop around — different banks offer different rates and packages.

4. Choose the Right Location

Location affects everything: price, rental yield, resale value, and your daily life. Ask yourself:

  • How close is the nearest MRT station?
  • Are there good schools within 1km?
  • What is the rental demand like in this area?
  • Is the neighbourhood up-and-coming or already mature?

Districts like District 9 (Orchard/River Valley), District 10 (Tanglin/Holland), and District 15 (East Coast) command premium prices but hold value well. Emerging areas like District 18 (Tampines/Pasir Ris) and District 23 (Bukit Panjang) offer better entry prices with growth potential.

5. New Launch vs. Resale

Each has its strengths. New launches offer modern designs, progressive payment schemes, and the excitement of being the first owner. Resale units let you move in immediately, see the actual unit, and negotiate on price.

For first-time buyers, resale often makes more sense because you can see exactly what you are getting and start living there right away. New launches work better if you have time to wait and want the latest specifications.

6. Work with the Right Agent

A good agent saves you time, money, and stress. They know the market, spot red flags, and negotiate on your behalf. At INITIUM, we guide first-time buyers through every step — from financing to keys.

Look for an agent who listens more than they talk, shows you units that actually fit your brief, and explains the paperwork without rushing you.

7. The Day of Completion

On completion day, your lawyer handles the transfer of funds, your bank releases the loan, and you collect the keys. Do a final inspection before signing anything. Check that all fittings, fixtures, and appliances listed in the sale agreement are present and working.
